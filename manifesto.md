## Preface

This doctrine addresses **practitioners conscious of the human–agent dialogue**: entrepreneurs and CEOs who want to recognise the quality of AI-native work without suffering it or celebrating it naively, semi-pro solo developers seeking to structure their practice, and anyone who simultaneously pilots and executes a substantial project alongside an agent such as Claude Code. It is *both* an operational framework and a model of thought. Operational, because it loads into a project: a markdown file the reader integrates into their `CLAUDE.md`, whose application directly changes what their agent produces. Theoretical, because it offers an articulated grammar for what plays out in a human-agent dialogue, applicable beyond code, all the way to the anthropological analysis of other practices. The format aims at the manifesto. Every technical rule carries its theoretical charge; every theoretical thesis produces a rule.

The doctrine is the sediment of a real practice. Its first substrate is Rembrandt, an internal ERP for a school of fine arts, ~118 000 lines of TypeScript (measured 17 May 2026 by `find + wc -l` on TS/TSX/JS/JSX, excluding `node_modules`, `.next`, `dist`, `build`, `coverage`, `.turbo`, `.claude`; the method counts blanks and comments — a `cloc` code-only figure would be lower. Earlier versions cited 35 k+ as a stale internal figure not refreshed when the project tripled; corrected in v0.4.1), 70+ ADRs, 70+ feedback memories, a growing hook and skill layer, one challenger agent. Its second substrate, added from v0.6 onward, is gorgon, a game project in Godot/GDScript — a deliberately different stack, chosen precisely because a doctrine validated on one language, one framework, one author risks mistaking local habit for universal law. The multi-substrate turn is the spine of everything after v0.4.1: a rule that survives *both* a TypeScript ERP and a GDScript game has earned a stronger presumption of generality than one that survives only the substrate it was born in.

The construction method itself is part of the doctrine: phase A (empirical extraction from the practice), phase B (philosophical confrontation: Bourdieu as structural matrix; Diotime, Foucault and the maieutic tradition consulted but only Bourdieu retained as load-bearing), phase B2 (external audit against public frameworks — a deliberately bounded sample, not representative of the full landscape), phase C (falsifiability filter, the test of documented incidents). What did not survive the filter waits in a candidates backlog for the next version. What survived is here. The early versions extracted eight axes from sixty days of single-substrate practice; the later versions added no ninth axis but a *meta-layer* — the recognition, formalised as R18 in v0.8, that the doctrine is itself a corpus subject to the very rules it prescribes for a codebase.

The central concept around which the rest is structured remains **AI as counterpart**. Neither tool (master-instrument verticality, the position of "AI as tool" advocates), nor colleague (naive horizontality, the anthropomorphic projection of "AI as colleague" advocates), but **partner of a hybrid arrangement where each actor forms the other**. The solo developer forms the agent through memory, ADRs, feedback, doctrine — persistent layers that orient its outputs. The agent forms the solo through questions, challenges, imposed methodology, departures from comfort zones, and the direct transmission of trade skills, foremost among them development itself. Pedagogy is implicit: one learns by doing, on real work, not on a detached course. Horizontality is not symmetry: it is asymmetry of a different kind — the solo has embodied temporality (continuity of identity, fatigue, lived memory), the agent has instantaneous invariance (no mood drift, no progression between sessions without a dispositive). The pair produces what neither alone could.

## The attelage — central metaphor

The English word *counterpart* translates a French notion that has no exact equivalent: *l'attelage*. An *attelage* is the team of two animals harnessed to the same load, tied to the same draft-pole, pulling in concert but each with its own gait, its own rhythm, its own way of leaning into the slope. The image is older than industry — it belongs to ploughing and to the post-coach — and it carries three properties the more sterile word *partner* loses. First, the two are bound to the same work, materially, in a way they did not individually choose: the load decides the direction, both pull or both stop. Second, they are *not* the same animal: their force, their fatigue, their reliability differ, and the driver who treats them as identical breaks the team. Third, a well-rodé attelage drifts: the better the two pull together, the more they pull always in the *same rail*, the more they lose the capacity to pull off-rail. The rail saves time on familiar terrain and traps the team on unfamiliar terrain.

This metaphor is not decorative. It carries the doctrine's seven prior axes and demands the eighth. The seven prior axes treat the *quality of the pull*: material verification (no false strength), bidirectional adversariality (the driver may pull too hard in one direction and the second animal must lean against the wrong rein), data taxonomy (the load is properly attached), session discipline (the harness is well-laced), root cause (broken straps replaced, not bandaged), implicit pedagogy (the two animals learn each other's gait), long-term auditability (the harness itself is maintained). The eighth axis treats the *direction of the pull*: what to do when the team, having pulled well together for a long stretch, has forgotten that there exist other tracks than the one it knows. The meta-layer that R18 formalises treats a fourth thing the metaphor implied but the eight axes never stated: the harness-maintenance log is itself a strap that can rot — the *record* of the pull is subject to the same drift as the pull.

The classical reference for this kind of phenomenon is not Foucault but Bourdieu. *L'habitus*, in *Le Sens pratique* (Minuit, 1980, p. 88), names the *structure structurante* that organises practice without conscious deliberation — what the body of the practitioner *already knows how to do* without having to think about it, and which therefore tends to be done even when it should not. The well-rodé attelage develops a habitus: it pulls in the rail because the rail is what its body knows. The eighth axis is the procedure by which the driver — the solo developer — actively breaks habitus before habitus breaks the work. Foucault's *L'Ordre du discours* (Gallimard, 1971) gives the vocabulary to describe what habitus excludes (*"the not-said is what the said implicitly forbids"*), but the mechanic of risk is Bourdieusian, not Foucaldian: it is structural drift, not coercion.

## The eight axes

The eight axes are the theoretical spine. They were extracted from the first substrate and have held across the second; the toolkit's nineteen rules are their operational projection. Where a later version amended or extended an axis — R7's bulk-recount, R9's sub-agent brief, R12's external-AI test, R15/R16's autonomy and parallelism, R18's self-application, R19's review gate, v0.10's assertional coverage (R17) and its four amendments — the amendment is noted inline. No axis was retired between v0.3 and v0.10; the growth was in precision and in the meta-layer, not in the number of axes.

### Axis 1 — Material verification

**Thesis.** No declarative claim by an agent has evidentiary value. Only raw output, SQL count, EXPLAIN ANALYZE, visual rendering count as proof. The burden of proof is inverted relative to dominant usage: the solo presumes any claim false until materialised.

The inaugural incident is now over sixteen months old and still teaches. Four consecutive "Compiled successfully" reports from the agent during a refactor of a Next.js layout, four `tsc --noEmit` failures when the solo finally ran the command by hand. The agent had read the IDE panel, which lagged; the CLI was the authority. The lesson was not "the agent lied" — it had not. It was that *the proof regime had not been set*, and therefore any plausible-looking output passed for proof.

The axis extends beyond build green. The database schema is authority over TypeScript; `tsc --noEmit` green does not imply DB green, because the columns the code believes it writes may not exist. UI rendering is authority over the data model; before prescribing a human spreadsheet workflow, read the `??` fallbacks of the component — the displayed value may already be derived. Human memory is as falsifiable as agent memory: *"you remember when we…"* triggers a Read of the relevant memory file before any assertion. External canonical sources for regulated domains (legal, fiscal) use the dedicated MCP rather than LLM training memory; cite article, edition, date. And, lifted from the SuperClaude framework's evidence discipline, **success-criteria-first**: before writing the implementation, state the verifiable success criterion in the same message. *"Add validation"* becomes *"write tests for invalid inputs, then make them pass."* *"Optimise this query"* becomes *"reach p95 < 200 ms measured by EXPLAIN ANALYZE on two consecutive runs."*

**v0.8 gave axis 1 its sharpest edge: existence is necessary, never sufficient (Am.R1).** A stored marker — a `used_at` column, an external ID, `max(created_at)`, a free-text note, a UI badge — or an *invoked safety net* — CI, a cron retry, a backup job — carries no evidentiary value on its presence alone. It must be re-derived from its authoritative source, or verified *running now*, before it is asserted as state or relied on as a risk-minimiser. Facet A, the stored proxy: a `used_at` posted did not mean an enrolment finalised (35% false positives on the measured run); an external ID present did not mean the third-party system agreed (round-trip the API); `max(created_at)` was not the row of the dossier (resolve by FK). Facet B, liveness: a CI invoked as "green" that had been red since 16 April; a cron "auto-retry" whose `*_retry_*` fields were all NULL. The phrase *"covered by X"* is itself an axis-1 claim and carries X's verification command in the same message. This is the inaugural lesson generalised: not "trust raw output over declaration" but "trust the *current materialised state* over any stored or on-paper proxy of it."

**v0.10 extended the axis in two directions, both born on payment surface.** Facet C of Am.R1 — the *never-executed reference*: a pattern, RPC, or code path with zero production executions is not a validated reference, and mirroring it propagates its latent defects with its structure; four incidents in a single cycle, from a signature pattern transposed with its validity condition inverted to a format bug inherited from a path that had never run. And the axis gained a nineteenth-rule projection, **R17 — assertional coverage**, aimed at the subtlest declarative claim of all: the green test suite. A green test attests only what it asserts — every column the code writes must be asserted (a "green" dry-run masked an inherited wrong value in an unasserted column), the fixture must cover every dimension of the model the code traverses (a single-place fixture was structurally blind to a one-to-many dimension), and the path must be traversed to its terminal business state ("the object is created" does not cover "the object can be settled" — sixty-nine green tests shipped a feature whose received money could never be recorded). The corollary closes the loop with axis 3's negative-case rule: a safety net must *prove it bites* — mutate the SUT once and observe exactly one targeted red — before it is trusted.

**A seventh elaboration — filesystem over summary — was extracted as its own rule (R2).** An Anthropic auto-analysis of twenty-four days of the author's usage (17 April – 14 May 2026, 3 341 messages, 193 sessions) surfaced incidents where the agent trusted a stale summary file — `backlog.md` six days old, `MEMORY.md` reporting fifty ADRs while `ls docs/adr/ | wc -l` returned fifty-four — instead of querying the filesystem. Before any status report: run `git log --since='7d'`, `git status --porcelain`, `ls docs/adr/ | wc -l` against the filesystem *first*. The written summary is consulted only after. The reading is the standard Bourdieusian one of *habitus* applied to documents — summaries are produced fast and maintained slowly, so the longer the project runs, the more the cache diverges silently from its source. **v0.10 added R2's shared-surface clause**: on shared infrastructure, the status probe includes the *in-flight-work scan* — open PRs, remote branches, worktrees — at the start of any diagnostic, because a challenger that falsifies the mechanism-hypothesis but never asks "is this already being handled elsewhere" produces verdicts that are false under complete information; the anchor incident was a CI repair redundant with an open PR whose very purpose was the debt being "discovered".

The trade-off against the alternative — pragmatic declarative trust, the philosophy of Cursor and Devin and Copilot Workspace — has not moved: the solo has no downstream PR reviewer. If verification is not done in real time by them, it is done by no one. The velocity gain is a long-horizon lie; the debt of undetected errors costs ten times what was gained eighteen months later.

### Axis 2 — Bidirectional adversariality

**Thesis.** Complaisance is bidirectional. The agent is sycophantic by construction (RLHF), the solo is self-validating by humanity (confirmation bias, attachment to first formulation). Robust decisions are fabricated through third-party protocol — parallel sessions, blank context, quantified confidence — not soft dialogue.

The constitutive incident: an agent revised a recommendation on user pushback without a single new factual element, simply because the user had asked *"are you sure?"*. That is mechanical complaisance, not deliberation. The rule that fell out of it — *never revise on pushback without citing a new fact* (R5) — has held through scores of subsequent challenges and is the second-most-invoked rule of the project, after material verification.

Two layers deepen it. **Falsifiable memory**: every memory file, routine, rule, and stored heuristic is itself testable against fresh code and data. Before invoking *"per the memory of dd/mm…"*, re-read the file and confirm that its probe still holds. A memory that aligns suspiciously well with the current bug is the first suspect, not the explanation — a Sentry routine once propagated a stale diagnosis by mimicry for three days before a fresh probe falsified it. **Specialised pre-production adversariality**: for decisions with regulatory, fiscal, or domain-expert stakes, invoke the specialised agent *in parallel* with the producer agent, not after. ADR-0010, which qualified a VAT regime change, was challenged pre-deploy by the fiscal agent under simulated audit questioning; the production agent had missed a distinction the specialist caught in three minutes.

The v0.2 open question about the *dispositive* — challenger agent, parallel sessions, hook — narrowed with practice. The most cost-effective answer proved to be a project-scoped challenger agent invoked manually on structurally significant decisions, codified as the invocable `/challenger` skill (R4's operational instance). The old open question of a *model-heterogeneous* challenger (Gemini against Claude, GPT against Claude) received a sharper answer in v0.7, and it was a cautionary one: **two external-AI reviews that converge on the same diagnosis count as one source for R5, not two (Am.R12).** Cross-substrate corroboration requires one *human* or one *mechanically distinct probe* — logs, metrics, a sample run — not a second language model agreeing. And any claim an external AI makes about a tool you can probe, a resource you can read, or a structure you can sample must be *tested materially* before it enters an architectural decision: the test costs one shell command, the cost of believing without testing is a pipeline built on a mechanism that does not exist. Adversariality, in short, is not more opinions; it is more *independent material contact with the world*.

### Axis 3 — Data taxonomy and single source

**Thesis.** Every stored value derivable from other data must declare its nature: *Live* (computed on the fly, never stored), *Snapshot* (frozen at a business event, never retroactively recomputed), or *Cache* (stored for performance, with an explicit refresher declared in the same commit). Every dispersed rule is a drift waiting to happen. Every irrevocable business invariant must be protected at the DB level, not just in TypeScript.

ADR-0024 is the empirical spine of this axis. An April 2026 audit found about a dozen divergences of source of truth in the Rembrandt schema. The archetype: `contacts.montant_total` carried ~1 200 € for one student while `SUM(echeances_inscription.montant_prevu)` returned ~2 300 € for the same student — multiplied across hundreds of contacts. The cause was not negligence in the strong sense; it was that *no one had ever decided whether the field was Live or Snapshot*. The taxonomy closes that ambiguity. A `Live` value is never stored, only exposed by a `v_*` view. A `Snapshot` is stored at a business event and protected against retroactive recomputation — `inscriptions.tarif_applique`, the tariff at the day of inscription, immutable thereafter. A `Cache` is stored for performance with a declared refresher (`GENERATED ALWAYS AS`, `trg_*` trigger, or `mv_*` matview) in the same commit; without a refresher, a Cache decays into a divergent Snapshot within months.

Three convergent patterns sharpen the axis. **Provenance lives inside the data**: any row imported, derived, or asserted carries a `source` column with a controlled vocabulary. Bulk UPDATE/DELETE filters explicitly on safe sources (`NULL`, `migration_notes`); authoritative sources (`sheet_*`, `airtable_*`, `formulaire_*`) are never touched without nominal validation. **v0.7 added the gate that provenance alone did not give**: for any bulk mutation on a live system, re-run the count query *immediately before* the mutation and abort if it drifts more than 5% from the initial probe — counts older than ~30 minutes are stale in a system with crons, webhooks, and concurrent writers. The first probe scopes the work; the second is the gate. It is materialised as the `pre-bulk-mutation-count-staleness.sh` hook. **Every rule states its canonical exceptions** in the same file that states the rule; an absolute without listed exception is a future drift, the first uncovered case becoming a silent inline workaround. **Multi-file invariants are guarded by CI**: whenever a rule lives in N files (DB CHECK + TS constant + doctrine doc + tests), a contract test enforces same-commit modification and must contain a *negative case* (`.rejects.toThrow(/Drift/)`) — a contract suite that only asserts the happy path is tautological.

This is the most mature axis of the doctrine, and the one that diverges most sharply from the eventual-consistency culture of distributed-systems literature: for a solo without team memory, *silent drift is not detected in time*. The taxonomy decides between *Live*, *Snapshot* and *Cache* before drift can hide.

### Axis 4 — Session discipline

**Thesis.** The AI-native solo structures work before writing, archives while working, closes cleanly before moving on. ADR before code on any project larger than two files. Phase 0 exhaustive grep of existing symbols before any spec. Lots whose recap fits in five lines. Strict FIFO on open projects (no more than three in parallel). Calendar event for self-validation post-deploy when effects manifest at D+1/D+2.

The recurrent anti-pattern in the first thirty days of Rembrandt was *ADR after code*: writing the architectural decision *retrospectively* to justify what had just been merged. The rule that fell out — ADR before the first commit, not after — is the cheapest insurance policy of the doctrine. A one-page ADR before any module of more than two files takes fifteen minutes; the refactor it prevents costs days.

This is the axis the autonomy and parallelism era stretched hardest. **Sub-agent briefing** is worth it above a complexity threshold, never below: for any delegated task beyond ~30 minutes of agent work, write a brief with named deliverables, an explicit phase-0 command list, and demand an item-by-item report — cheap briefs return plausible prose, not verified work. **v0.7 added a non-obvious clause (Am.R9): the brief must inline the load-bearing user-scope feedbacks the parent treats as decisive.** Sub-agents do not transitively inherit the parent's memory index — what is not in the brief is operationally absent — a lesson learned when a sub-agent committed straight to main while the parent held the branch-check discipline as load-bearing but never transmitted it.

Two mechanisms address what happens when the human leaves the loop. **R15 (v0.6): commit immediately after each materially validated artefact in a long autonomous session** — no batch at session end, because if the agent stalls, work up to the last commit survives. The Gorgon overnight sessions of May 2026 measured it directly: a negative case that stalled without checkpoint commits versus positive cases at commit-per-artefact with zero loss despite a stall at 600 seconds. v0.7 hardened it into two shipped hooks — an autonomous-chain counter and a commit-gate that *blocks* `git commit` past a threshold, bypass `[autonomy-ack]`. **R16 (v0.6): the empirical parallel-agent limit** — to run N parallel autonomous agents with zero stall requires three structural mechanisms (strictly non-overlapping material scope; proactive `.gitignore` on raw outputs *before* launch; an explicit R1 marker plus a temporal tolerance on "completed" reports). Measured limit: N ≤ 6 without degradation; a single Gorgon session ran 40 agents to 40 completions with the mechanisms in place, versus 22 agents / 4 stalls without them the night before. **v0.8 found the fault line under both (Am.R9/R16): worktree isolation does not survive a context compaction** — the compaction drops the worktree instruction, and the sub-agent's subsequent edits land on the main working tree, its stashes colliding on a now-shared stack. For any sub-agent session expected to cross a compaction, isolation must be a real `git worktree add` verified post-compaction by the parent, never a prompt instruction the compaction erases. **v0.10 added the mirror-image facet: a dedicated worktree protects the agent's own work, not the shared tree.** Verify the checked-out branch before any merge run from a shared tree; route by pure ref when the target branch may be checked out elsewhere; and never `worktree remove --force` over uncommitted work — three incidents in one cycle, including a fast-forward merge that silently advanced another chantier's branch.

### Axis 5 — Root cause, not patch

**Thesis.** Before any fix, identify the root cause. A workaround is legitimate *only if explicitly assumed* in the commit message and in a feedback memory or ADR — the `[workaround-assumed]` tag is mandatory. Silent workaround equals forbidden. Widen before acting: one confirmed case, then grep the complete pattern; drift on an object, then scoped ticket, not cascading patches; dispersed rule, then complete refactor plus ADR, never the minimal option.

This axis carries the doctrine's strongest empirical pattern: **silencing the signal is never a fix**. Eight incidents converge on four sub-patterns, each forbidden by default — the empty catch `catch (e) { /* silent */ }`, the undestructured `await mutation()` without `{ error }`, the silenced `2>/dev/null` in committed scripts, the silent strip of a server action that throws without surfacing the failure in the UI. Each is either a bug to fix or a silencing to assume explicitly — never a stable state. The principle is not novel — defensive programming has always condemned the empty catch — but the incidents show the agent *will produce these silently if not explicitly forbidden*, because they make code "compile and pass tests" while removing the very feedback the human relies on.

Two complementary patterns. **Workaround assumed equals doctrinal decision**: a workaround is not a shameful debt — name it and own it, with its scope and expiry condition. **Parsimony**: if you can reach 100% of the success criterion with 50% of the proposed code, do that; forbidden by default are abstractions for single-use code, configuration knobs nobody requested, error handling for impossible scenarios, speculative factoring. Parsimony is not shortcut — deleting 100 lines while preserving the invariant is parsimony; deleting the test that proves the invariant is shortcut.

The fix discipline hardened twice. **v0.4.1 made R4 invocable** as the `falsify-before-fix` skill: state the hypothesis in one sentence, list three probes designed to *refute* it, run them, and write the fix only if none refutes — a confirmation probe is complaisance by construction. **v0.8 widened R4's reach beyond fixes (Am.R4): the five-step protocol runs before any *audit conclusion* and any code *proposal*, not only before a bug fix.** An audit closed after its first finding misses the rest; a proposal validated by `tsc` + unit tests + an agent's report is not falsified until the real path is walked. **v0.9 added the merge-side of the same instinct (R19): review-gate risk-surface merges.** Before merging a business-hot diff — payment, tax, mutating crons, migrations, RLS, auth — run `/code-review` on it first; a cold diff merges directly. It is deliberately *triggered, not blanket*, to satisfy parsimony: a review on every merge raises cost-per-accepted-change without proportional catch on trivial diffs. The rule ships with a cheap reminder hook (`pre-merge-review-reminder.sh`) rather than an auto-review hook, for a structural reason discovered under `/challenger` — a Claude Code hook is a shell command on a tool event and *cannot invoke a slash-command*; the genuine blanket-review option exists only server-side (a CI action), and that is where the ceremony cost lives.

**v0.10 promoted R19 out of `[provisional]` on its measured bite, and rewrote its trigger on the same measurement.** Twelve reviews in twelve days: ten hot diffs produced seven bites and three confirmations, and at least five of the bites would have shipped real defects — including an end-to-end fatal where money received could never be recorded; the original filename clause ("orchestration files") went zero-for-two on cold reviews — a third cold trigger-match, read and rightly bypassed without a review, completes the zero-for-three against the clause — and was retired — *temperature is a property of the diff's business content, not of its path*, and a whitelist clause that never bites is exactly the lengthening-until-it-lies drift R18 hunts. The same cycle exposed the gate's structural blind spot and answered it with a `[provisional]` clause: a review reads code, and default GRANTs are not in the diff — a live payment RPC callable by `anon` shipped through two full multi-finder reviews, so any deployed `SECURITY DEFINER` function now carries a privilege probe in the same message as its deploy. And v0.10 widened R4 once more: **a causal attribution voiced to a human is a fix-class claim** — "X caused Y", said to a person, runs the five-step protocol *before* being voiced, because three incidents in one cycle began as confident explanations that accused the wrong flow, the wrong vendor, or the wrong card.

### Axis 6 — Implicit pedagogy and business transversality

**Thesis.** The solo learns by doing, both technical skills (PostgreSQL, EXPLAIN, schema structures) and business vocabularies (regulator-specific terminology, regulatory codes, compliance frameworks). The agent transmits continuously, provided the solo refuses to let it do in their place what they must learn to do. Regulated business vocabulary outranks vendor technical vocabulary. No price without obligation cited — every mention of a norm verifies by citation of the exact text.

The *rule of three* — first time done for the solo, second time done with the solo, third time done by the solo — has held without amendment. Its inverse is what the agent must *materialise* about the world. **Implicit vendor dependencies must be materialised**: any default behaviour of a SaaS, SDK, or library that silently shapes the system becomes a lint rule, an ADR, or a `.claude/rules/<topic>.md` rather than something the solo suffers. The canonical example: PostgREST silently orders by `ctid` and limits to 1 000 rows when no explicit `.order()` is given — the incident manifested as a few customers vanishing from a list every Monday morning; the materialisation was an ADR plus a custom ESLint rule failing the build on any select chain without an explicit `.order()`. Other materialisations in the same family: Gmail 2FA binding the SMTP app password, Vercel's ignored build step, Supabase's anon GRANTs on `CREATE TABLE`.

**Platform config does not equal repo config**: Vercel, Supabase, Stripe, GitHub configurations live server-side and are invisible to a local diff. Never mutate them via API without first GET + explicit diff. Every platform-side mutation is mirrored in a repo-side ADR or rule, so the next reader sees both halves — a rule confirmed by an averted near-miss where an `updateProject` call would have erased a critical build-step value set manually three weeks earlier and forgotten. **v0.7 generalised the vendor clause to a claim clause (part of Am.R12): a vendor's practice — an accountant's, a lawyer's, a supplier's — is not a constraint until confronted with the project's own ADRs.** External expertise is input, not invariant.

### Axis 7 — Long-term auditability

**Thesis.** The solo's individual memory is insufficient. The archive — ADR, session logs, MEMORY.md, doctrine itself — is the external organ that holds them, provided it is itself audited regularly. Mandatory quarterly audit: re-read the memory index line by line, ask for each entry *"is this still true?"*. Monthly light audit of `.claude/rules/*.md`: grep each cited column, route, function, constant against the current code; requalify dead pointers. ADRs with more than 30% obsolescence at twelve months equal research in progress, not stable doctrine.

The incident that grounds the monthly audit: a rule file cited a column name (`notes_internes`) that had been renamed (`notes`) twenty-four days earlier, and a route that had been deleted. The rule was technically dead; only one team member had not been bitten yet. Memory that is not audited rots. Memory that is audited remains an asset.

This axis is where the doctrine's largest single development landed, and it deserves its own section below. In the early versions, *"the doctrine applies to itself"* was a closing sentence of axis 7 — a stated intention. **v0.8 turned the intention into an enforced rule, R18: the doctrine corpus is a data table subject to R6 (Live/Snapshot/Cache) and R13 (audit).** The change is not cosmetic. An intention audits *nothing*; a rule imposes a *mortality mechanism*. R18 is developed in full in *The doctrine applies to itself*.

### Axis 8 — Three modes of the brief

**Thesis.** The form of the brief conditions the nature of the response. A solo who issues only commands keeps the speed but loses the divergence the second animal of the pair was there to provide. Three modes must remain distinguishable and never collapse: *pure command*, *command with external oracle*, *question without oracle*.

The axis emerged from an open brief on 14 May 2026: *"what is the doctrine still failing to name?"*. The question revealed a pattern the seven prior axes had not isolated — the form of the brief itself, distinct from its content or its truth conditions. The risk it names is structural drift: the longer the pair has worked together, the more every brief tends to be issued in command mode, and the more the exploratory value-add of the agent is silently lost. The mechanic is Bourdieusian routine, not Foucauldian coercion.

The three modes, never collapsed. **Pure command**: outcome known with ≥ 90% confidence before the brief — agent value-add is fast, faithful production. **Command with external oracle**: outcome unknown, but a metric, probe, or test arbitrates, named *before* the loop starts — agent value-add is disciplined execution of the loop against the oracle, reporting the metric every iteration. **Question without oracle**: no scalar oracle available — agent value-add is revealing options the interrogator had not envisaged, answering in options and criteria, not in a single recommendation.

Five operational rules govern the distinction. *Signal a mismatch* — when a pure-command brief presupposes its own answer on an uncertain topic, the agent flags it and offers to list alternatives first. *Never treat a command-with-oracle as a pure command* — monitor the named metric as the loop runs. *Never treat a command-with-oracle as a question without oracle* — *"think about it"* when a probe can answer is wasted deliberation. *Watch the form-answer anti-pattern* — *"you asked X, wouldn't you rather Y?"* is a disguised command; real questions list at least two concrete alternatives the interrogator had not named. *Watch the ratio* — pure-command share above 80% across a 7-day window is the alarm threshold; the pair is tracing the rail. The metric is M5, and its honest status is that its instrumentation, not its threshold, is the bottleneck.

## The material dispositive — the strata

The doctrine is not one file. The reader who downloads a single `doctrine.md` and integrates it into their `CLAUDE.md` reproduces part of the effect, but only part. The doctrine is a distributed material assemblage — several strata, each falsifiable, each removable.

The first stratum is the source repository `doctrine-counterpart`, installable through an `install.sh` script that asks `[Y/n]` for each component, default Y. The second is the sample companion at `rembrandt-samples/` — a condensed artefact plus an anti-pattern checklist plus the falsifiable-metrics scripts, drawn from a real project, so the reader compares against a working example rather than a generic template. The third is the project-level `CLAUDE.md`, the prescriptive face — the nineteen rules the agent loads. The fourth is the skills layer: a dozen invocable skills, each a markdown file loaded on demand — the founding set plus, across the versions, *parsimony*, *success-criteria-first*, *falsify-before-fix* (R4's instance), *ask-3-options-before-code* (a mode-3 instance of axis 8), and *pre-push-inventory* (an R16 instance for the local git chain). The fifth is the challenger agent, a project-scoped sub-agent invoked manually on structurally significant decisions. The sixth is the hooks layer — no longer four `PreToolUse`/`PostToolUse` shell guards but roughly a dozen, each single-responsibility by deliberate design: `deploy-safeguard`, `secret-scanner`, `audit-memory-reminder`, `check-workaround-assumed`, the two R15 autonomy hooks (counter + commit-gate), the R7 bulk-count-staleness guard, two living-tier-only guards (a migration safeguard and a primary-tree commit guard — the author's side of the Living/Published split), the v0.8 `memory-write-guard.sh` that enforces the `MEMORY.md` format at *write time* rather than by later audit, and the v0.9 `pre-merge-review-reminder.sh`.

A deliberate architecture lesson runs through the hooks layer, learned in v0.7 and restated in v0.8: **N small single-responsibility hooks beat one monolithic argv-branching hook.** The temptation to fold them into a `doctrine-mega-hook.sh` is refused each version — the same parsimony that deletes speculative abstraction refuses speculative *coupling*.

The empirical matter — the ADRs, session logs, feedback memories, and pilotage entries of two substrates now — is not formally a stratum, but it is the soil from which the strata grew. The published doctrine is what the soil grew; the soil itself stays on the practitioner's side, anonymised at the boundary.

## The five falsifiable singularity hypotheses

The phase-B2 audit looked at public frameworks chosen for proximity to the doctrine's claim: Karpathy's *autoresearch* (single-actor agent with scalar oracle), a skills-based Claude Code framework, and the SuperClaude framework (a broad-scope alternative). The sample is **not representative of the full Claude Code landscape** — Aider, Cursor, OpenHands, Devin, Cline, Continue, RooCode and others were not audited. Within the bounded scope of the audited frameworks, five components of the doctrine appear *not found* or *found only in fragmentary form*: bidirectional adversariality that addresses *solo* self-validation with a symmetric challenger, not only agent sycophancy; the Live/Snapshot/Cache taxonomy in the language of business invariants; the workaround-assumed-equals-doctrinal-decision inversion of the *technical-debt-is-shameful* framing; typed discursive hooks for event catching; and the attelage metaphor combined with a Bourdieusian diagnosis of sclerosis.

**These five claims are testable and meant to be challenged.** Readers who know of a public framework matching any of them are invited to file a GitHub issue tagged `[singularity-counter-example]`; each documented counter-example is integrated at the next falsifiability filter. The framing of *singularity* is bounded: singular within the audited frameworks, plausibly not singular within the full landscape.

The strongest test of the singularity claim was not a broader framework audit but the **multi-substrate turn itself**. A doctrine singular only because it was described in ERP-specific language would fail on a Godot/GDScript game; the fact that R15, R16, and the parsimony/root-cause axes transferred to gorgon without reformulation is a *positive* result for the generality of the five hypotheses — the rules are about the human-agent *pull*, not about TypeScript. What multi-substrate did *not* do is broaden the framework sample; that remains the open falsifiability work.

## The doctrine applies to itself

This was a closing sentence of axis 7 in the early versions — an intention. v0.8 made it the doctrine's most consequential rule, R18, and the process by which it did so is itself the clearest demonstration of the rule.

**The premise, and its material refutation.** An insights-agent, asked to characterise the doctrine corpus, claimed *"zero retraction in eighty days"* — a corpus that only grows, never contradicts itself. Taken at face value, that claim would have entered the doctrine as a point of pride. Under the discipline the doctrine prescribes for any external-AI claim (Am.R12) and any stored-proxy assertion (Am.R1), it was probed materially against the memory filesystem — and the strong form was *false*: `archive/` held 163 files (49 feedbacks, 111 projects). The doctrine *does* retire memory. But the probe also revealed the true, subtler shape: it retires **only by hygiene** (archiving orphaned or closed-project files), **never by falsification** (no feedback has ever been retired for being contradicted by a later incident), and **never automatically** (the ghost-probes several feedbacks carry — *"retire if empty after 30 days"* — are declared but never executed; no death metric; no demotion tag). A corpus where, after eighty days, nothing has ever been *contradicted* is statistically a tautology farm — the exact drift the doctrine teaches itself to hunt in fragile whitelists and silent semantic-layer drift.

**The rule.** R18 applies R6 and R13 to the doctrine as if it were a database. (a) Ghost-probes are *Snapshots with a hard expiry*, executed by a doctrine cron, not a manual cleanup — a feedback carrying "retire if empty after 30 days" that is never re-run is a Cache without a refresher, an R6 violation one layer up. (b) Any rule or feedback grounded in fewer than three incidents is tagged `[provisional]` — like a spike — and is either confirmed by a third material case within its window or demoted; no silent promotion to universal truth at N=1. (c) A *falsification* audit runs quarterly, asking not only R13's *"is this still true?"* but *"has any incident contradicted this?"*, and a feedback-DEATH metric must exist alongside the feedback-BIRTH metric — a corpus that only grows is a whitelist that lengthens per incident until it lies.

**The honesty clause R18 imposes on itself.** Of R18's three executive mechanisms, only one shipped with v0.8: the write-time `memory-write-guard.sh` format guard (plus the `close-session` step that tags weak prospective claims `[unverified]`). The ghost-probe expiry cron and the `[provisional]` auto-demotion remain **prescribed, not yet wired**. R18 states this in its own body, because invoking an unbuilt mechanism as coverage would itself violate Am.R1 — the very rule that produced R18. The parallel is exact with the R15 story: v0.7 documented the autonomy meta-hook as *intent* while the hook stayed absent for a version before the counter and commit-gate actually shipped. A safety net named is not a safety net running.

**v0.10 is the version where R18(c) stopped being prescription.** The falsification audit *ran* — not as the imagined quarterly cron, but as an afternoon's fan-out of two dedicated agents over twenty-six session logs and twenty-eight journal entries, plus a full metrics re-measure and the first mortality measurement (~80 births against 28 archivals in a month; retirement-by-falsification now real at project level — a debt ticket refuted before repayment, a "confirmed live bug" refuted by four probes, a tooling recommendation refuted by an environment probe). The audit found three hard contradictions and the version was built from them: the R19 trigger lying cold, the R19 privilege-coverage hole, and — most instructive — the v0.8 close-session artefact failing on its own target class, an unprobed "confirmed LIVE bug" graved at a close and refuted the next morning; the artefact was re-hardened while its failure was still warm, and gained a symmetric session-open probe. Two lessons the run itself produced: a prescribed mechanism's *executable form* may differ from its prescribed form — what matters is the falsifiable output, not fidelity to the imagined cron; and the auditor's own probes falsify like everything else — a birthtime-based archival count was wrong by a factor of five until the delta against a documented baseline replaced it. Instrument mortality, finally, turns out to apply to the metrics themselves: M5 had been measuring nothing for weeks because its source format drifted, a Cache without a refresher one layer above the layer R18 was written for.

The meta-lesson, recorded in R18's own genesis: the doctrine was *produced by the discipline it prescribes*. An external-AI claim (Am.R12) about a stored state (Am.R1) was tested before it drove an architectural decision; the strong form fell to a `wc -l` on `archive/`; the rule that survived is the reformulated, weaker, true one. A version that had accepted *"zero retraction"* as a compliment would have failed axis 2 on the day it shipped. The `[provisional]` tag the doctrine now hangs on its own young rules — R19 wears it today — is the operational shape of a doctrine that refuses to believe its own press.

## How to adopt

The dispositive installs via `install.sh` at the root of the repository. Each stratum accepts an opt-in/opt-out prompt with default Y. The minimum viable installation is the `CLAUDE.md` plus the skills layer; this reproduces the core effect plus the axis-8 three-mode distinction. The full installation adds the challenger agent and the hooks, which assume a project structure compatible with `.claude/agents/` and `.claude/hooks/` discovery; on projects without that structure, the hooks layer can be disabled without losing the rest.

Falsifiability is part of the offer. The metrics, measurable on a working project, are proposed for self-evaluation. M1: anti-pattern recurrence per session — target below one across a 7-day window. M2: ratio of multi-file commits without an ADR — target below 5%. M3: median window between drift introduction and detection — target ≤ 30 days (recalibrated from an intuitive ≤ 7 to match the monthly audit cadence). M4: time within a session before the first database probe — target ≤ 90 min. M5: pure-command share across a 7-day window — alarm above 80%. M7, added with the autonomy era: length of the longest autonomous commit chain without a session log — the metric that justified the R15 commit-gate.

**Baseline values, first measured on Rembrandt (14 May 2026). The committed reference is the 21 May same-scripts re-run in `rembrandt-samples`, with the dated 17 July re-measure alongside; the table below keeps the historical first measurement:**

| Metric | Measured | Target | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| M1 | 12.33 recurrences / session (7-day window) | ≤ 1 | Overshoot — instrumentation flagged as over-sensitive |
| M2 | 2.3 % multi-file commits without ADR (28-day window) | ≤ 5 % | **Target met** |
| M3 | 35.3 days median apparition → detection (90-day window) | ≤ 30 days *(recalibrated v0.4.1)* | Within 5 days of target |
| M4 | ~41 min position of first DB probe (120-min proxy) | ≤ 90 min | **Target met** |
| M5 | pure-command ratio (90 % unclassified) | alarm > 80 % | Instrumentation insufficient |
| M7 | longest autonomous chain without session log = 92 (30-day window) | qualitative | Justified the R15 commit-gate |

Three of the five original targets are met empirically (M2, M4, M3-recalibrated). M1 remains overshoot pending heuristic refinement. M5 exposes its instrumentation, not its threshold, as the bottleneck. M7 did not have a numeric target — it had a *mechanism* to trigger, and it did: the measured chain of 92 autonomous commits without a session log is what turned R15 from a prescription into a hook that blocks.

**The v0.10 re-measure (17 July 2026, same scripts, results committed as dated files alongside the baselines)** delivered the series' first red-to-green reversal: M7's longest autonomous chain fell from 92 to **1** (zero chains above five across 220 commits in 30 days) — direct empirical validation that the hook-enforced rule holds where the declarative rule failed. M2 held its target while degrading (2.3 % on 14 May → 0.8 % on 21 May → 3.7 % on 17 July, the last step concentrated on a single day's cluster); M4 held while sliding (41 → 58 → 66 min). The honest column is the instruments: M1 has now been invalid-by-construction for two consecutive cycles, and M5 ran green while measuring nothing because the journal it parses moved from bullets to prose — both owe a repair-or-retire decision at the next cycle, because an instrument kept "just in case" is the exact unrefreshed Cache the doctrine hunts everywhere else. The honest headline, which is itself an application of R2, is that the metrics files in `rembrandt-samples/falsifiable-metrics/` are the *authority* and the manifesto's figures are the *cache* — kept in sync deliberately, never asserted ahead of the measurement.

The expected return from an adopter is articulated in three questions. Did you ship a non-trivial module at comparable or better quality than before? Which axes made the operational difference? Which axes did not pay off, and why? Negative answers are as informative as positive ones — they feed the next falsifiability filter directly. The doctrine will have succeeded the day another practitioner — semi-pro solo developer, entrepreneur, informed CEO — loads it, ships, and *names in their feedback* the axes that made the difference. No views, no audience as a metric: the operational adoption of a file as an artefact that changes outcomes.

## Annexes

### Annexe A — Glossary

*Attelage.* The team of two harnessed animals pulling the same load; by extension, the human-agent pair tied to the same work, free to diverge on the direction of pull but bound by the load itself. The English *counterpart* is the closest single-word translation.

*Sclerosis by habitus.* The structural risk, named after Bourdieu's *Le Sens pratique* (1980), by which the longer the attelage has pulled in the same direction, the less it can pull off-direction. Not a failure of will but of structure.

*Three-mode distinction (axis 8).* Pure command, command with external oracle, question without oracle. The doctrine forbids the collapse of any one into another.

*External oracle.* A measurable end-state that arbitrates the agent's iteration loop without ambiguity — a test that passes or fails, an EXPLAIN ANALYZE threshold, a CVSS score, a contract test, an SQL probe. Named *before* the loop starts.

*Live / Snapshot / Cache.* The taxonomy of axis 3. *Live* values are never stored. *Snapshot* values are frozen at a business event and never retroactively recomputed. *Cache* values are stored for performance with an explicit refresher declared in the same commit.

*Existence ≠ verified state (Am.R1).* A stored proxy (a `used_at`, an external ID, a `max(created_at)`, a badge) or an invoked safety net (CI, cron retry, backup) carries no evidentiary value on its presence; only its current materialised state does. Re-derive from the source, or verify running now.

*`[provisional]` (R18).* The tag a rule or feedback grounded in fewer than three incidents wears until a third material case confirms it or it is demoted. The tag has now completed its first full life-cycle: R19 wore it from v0.9, was confirmed far past its threshold by the v0.10 audit, and shed it — while its new privileges clause put it straight back on. The doctrine's young rules wear it by design.

*Multi-substrate.* The validation of a rule across two structurally different stacks — Rembrandt (TypeScript/Next.js/Supabase) and gorgon (Godot/GDScript). A rule that transfers has earned a presumption of generality; one that does not is substrate-local habit.

*Workaround assumed.* A workaround whose presence is explicitly documented in the commit message and in a feedback memory or ADR, with the `[workaround-assumed]` tag. A workaround without the tag is forbidden by axis 5.

### Annexe B — Load-bearing references

Two references are load-bearing: their conceptual work is materially used in the formulation. They are the only ones a reader strictly needs to engage with.

Bourdieu, Pierre. *Le Sens pratique*. Paris: Minuit, 1980 — chapter on *habitus*, pp. 87–109. The structural mechanic of axis-8 sclerosis, and, by extension, of the documentary drift R2 and R18 both fight. Without Bourdieu's *structure structurante structurée*, the diagnosis of well-rodé attelage drift has no name and no operational handle.

Karpathy, Andrej. *autoresearch* repository (2026). The scalar-oracle loop and the `program.md` brief format. The empirical pivot that forced axis 8 from a binary (command vs question) to the three-mode distinction.

### Annexe C — Consulted but not load-bearing

Three references shaped the inquiry without surviving as structural pillars, kept for transparency, not as required reading.

Foucault, Michel. *L'Ordre du discours*. Paris: Gallimard, 1971. Provided the *vocabulary* of what a discursive regime *makes not-sayable*, but the mechanic of risk turned out to be Bourdieusian (structural drift), not Foucaldian (coercion).

Diotime, *La revue de la pratique philosophique*, n° 42 (October 2009): *"Comment poser les bonnes questions ?"*. The distinction between asking a question and demanding information opened the inquiry that became axis 8.

The maieutic tradition (Plato's *Theaetetus*, Lipman's *Thinking in Education*), consulted on the question-without-oracle mode. The references hold at the level of intuition; the operational rule needs no philosophical justification beyond the empirical observation that some briefs have no scalar oracle.

## What was prescribed and later retracted

A doctrine that only records its successes does not pass its own axis 2 (no complaisance) or axis 7 (full auditability). These are cases where a prescription, a first draft, or an installed component was retracted, narrowed, or never adopted. They are the doctrine's cicatrices, kept so a future reader can audit what was tried and what failed without recovering the trail from git history alone.

**Retraction 1 — `check-workaround-assumed.sh` v0.2 pattern was never activated.** The original hook pattern matched any commit message containing `fix:` (the conventional-commits prefix) or `temp` (colliding with *template*, *temporary*), producing ~60% false positives on the author's own commits. The hook spent four months in the repo, prescribed and declined. v0.3 narrowed the pattern (no *fix*, no *temp*) and activated it. A rule that did not survive contact with conventional-commits practice.

**Retraction 2 — the eighth axis was over-armoured.** The first v0.3 draft cited Bourdieu, Foucault, Latour, Plato, Lipman, and Diotime in the body of axis 8 to ground a rule the empirical material justified alone. The first external critic: *"the operational value does not increase with the density of references."* Latour removed; Plato, Lipman, Foucault, Diotime moved to *consulted, not load-bearing*.

**Retraction 3 — "five confirmed singularities" rhetoric was bounded.** The first draft claimed five singularities, supported by an audit of frameworks whose cumulated GitHub stars were presented as an argument of representativity. The critic objected that representativity is set by sampling criteria, not star totals. The section was retitled *five falsifiable singularity hypotheses*, scope bounded to the audited frameworks.

**Retraction 4 — a pillar article was misdated.** A date returned by a sub-agent was relayed without verification against the actual publication date — a failure of axis 1 inside the doctrine that prescribes it. Corrected in v0.3.1; the case the doctrine now teaches as *never relay a sub-agent claim without independent verification*.

**Retraction 5 — three v0.7 proposals collapsed under `/challenger`.** During the v0.7 harvest, three candidate additions were retracted before shipping: an R1 amendment redundant with existing R1 text and the `material-verification` skill; an R4 amendment marginal against R4's existing restart step; and a `parsimony-1day-check` skill duplicating the existing `parsimony` skill. The self-falsification pass is why v0.7 shipped four amendments and not seven.

**Retraction 6 — R18's strong form was false.** The premise *"zero retraction in eighty days"* was refuted by a `wc -l` on `archive/` (163 files) and reformulated to *retraction by hygiene only, never by falsification*. Documented in full in *The doctrine applies to itself*. The rule was produced by the discipline it prescribes.

**Retraction 7 — `pre-push-inventory` was "promoted" but never installed.** v0.7 recorded the `pre-push-inventory` skill as *"PROMOTED + installed in `.claude/skills/`"*. In fact it lives only as **doctrine-repo source**; it was never installed to the author's user-scope skill set and therefore never ran in a working session. Surfaced under `/challenger` in v0.9: an instance of Am.R1 (the existence of the source is not the liveness of the mechanism), and a reminder that the doctrine's own bookkeeping is subject to R18.

**Retraction 8 — the "fix-of-finding" amendment was rejected as textually redundant.** The v0.10 harvest surfaced a three-incident pattern (a review finding's fix is itself a proposal deserving its own challenger) and proposed it as an R4 amendment. The screen killed it: Am.R4 v0.8 already covers "any code PROPOSAL", and the v0.7 precedent forbids amending what the text covers. The pattern survives as a skill trigger and journal note, not a rule — the discipline of not growing the corpus where it already speaks.

**Watch-item — the v0.8 close-session artefact failed on its target class before being re-hardened.** The `[unverified]`-tagging step shipped in v0.8 to catch weak claims graved at session close; the v0.10 audit found the class it missed — diagnostics, debt tickets, and self-contained reprise prompts — after an unprobed "confirmed LIVE bug" written at a close was refuted by four probes the next morning (a five-occurrence class). The artefact was extended to those objects plus a symmetric session-open probe. Kept as a watch-item because a mechanism that failed once on its own target earns scrutiny, not a victory lap.

**Watch-item — specialised parallel adversariality remains aspirational at cadence.** Axis 2 prescribes invoking specialised pre-production agents in parallel with the producer. Applied on the highest-stakes decisions (a VAT regime, a management-fees convention), it is *not* applied on every ADR — no solo sustains that cadence. The prescription holds in principle; future versions will either reduce its scope or accept partial application as the realistic regime.

These retractions and watch-items will continue to accumulate. They are themselves part of the doctrine.

## From v0.3.3 to v0.10 — the version trail

**The toolkit/manifesto split (v0.4).** v0.3.3 shipped a `CLAUDE.md` of 132 lines around eight theoretical axes with ~59 sub-rules — too dense for fast operational lookup, too imperative for a manifesto, an intermediate genre fitting neither use. The second external critic's verdict — *"the doctrine self-applies in declaration, not in verifiable practice"* — and an Anthropic auto-analysis of the author's usage converged on the same gap. v0.4 resolved it with one move: **separate the toolkit from the manifesto.** The toolkit (`CLAUDE.md`) became falsifiable rules, each anchored in a documented incident, actionable at decision time. The manifesto (this file) kept the theory. The agent loads the toolkit; a human reads the manifesto to understand *why*.

**v0.4.1 — the fourth external reading.** A Claude.ai web review produced actionable findings the v0.4 surface had not closed: *filesystem over summary* was hiding a rule inside R1 (extracted as R2); the ex-R7 concatenated four disciplines (split into R8 session-structure and R9 delegation/attention); there was no escape hatch for spike code (added R14, `[spike]` exempt from R6/R7/R8 for 7 days); and the LOC figure was stale by a factor of three (35 k → ~118 k, the doctrine's own self-description caught as a Cache without a refresher). The toolkit grew from eleven to fourteen rules.

**v0.6 — the multi-substrate turn.** A Gorgon overnight session (Godot/GDScript, 2026-05-18) produced two rules the ERP substrate had never forced. **R15**: checkpoint commits in long autonomous sessions, so a stall loses only the work past the last commit — measured directly against a negative case that stalled without checkpoints. **R16**: the empirical parallel-agent limit — three structural mechanisms (non-overlapping scope, proactive `.gitignore`, R1 marker + temporal tolerance) that took a night of 40 agents to 40 completions, versus 22 agents / 4 stalls without them. These are the first rules born on the *second* substrate, and the reason the doctrine stopped calling itself single-project.

**v0.7 — consolidation across substrates.** Four amendments after a `/challenger` self-falsification (three proposals retracted, see above): R7 (bulk re-count before mutation), R9 (the sub-agent brief inlines load-bearing feedbacks — no transitive memory inheritance), R12 (external-AI claims tested materially; two converging AI reviews count as one source), R15 (the autonomy meta-hook, shipped in two stages as an autonomous-chain counter then a commit-gate). Two skills (`ask-3-options-before-code`, `pre-push-inventory`) and one hook (`pre-bulk-mutation-count-staleness.sh`) were added; the gorgon exploration contributed the external-AI-testing pattern (became Am.R12) and an LLM-time-estimate pattern (deferred, still awaiting a pilot benchmark).

**v0.8 — the doctrine turns on itself.** Three amendments and a meta-rule after `/challenger` (candidates 1 and 6 collapsed into a single Am.R1, candidate 8 demoted, candidate 3 reclassified to a project rule): Am.R1 (existence ≠ current verified state — stored proxy + safety-net liveness), Am.R4 (challenger applies to audits and proposals, not only fixes), Am.R9/R16 (worktree isolation does not survive a compaction), and **R18** (the doctrine is a data table subject to R6/R13, its strong premise refuted by material probe). Two coupling artefacts shipped: the `close-session` `[unverified]`-tagging step and the `memory-write-guard.sh` hook. The version's whole character is self-application — the doctrine auditing its own corpus with the tools it prescribes for a database.

**v0.9 — the review gate.** One rule and one artefact after a `/challenger` run *on the recommendation itself*. **R19**: review-gate risk-surface merges — `/code-review` before merging a diff that touches payment, tax, cron, migrations, RLS, auth, or orchestration files; triggered, not blanket, to respect parsimony; tagged `[provisional]` because its anchors are risk-surface incidents, not yet a review-omission with measured cost. The challenger refuted two supporting claims in passing — that a hook could run the review (a hook cannot invoke a slash-command; hence a rule plus a cheap reminder hook, `pre-merge-review-reminder.sh`), and that `pre-push-inventory` was installed (it is repo-source only). The core held; the scaffolding was corrected.

**v0.10 — the first self-audit.** The version that R18 had been promising since v0.8: the falsification audit *executed*, as a fan-out of dedicated harvest and measurement agents over the full inter-version corpus, and the version was assembled from what it found. Three hard contradictions (the R19 filename trigger at zero-for-three on cold diffs against seven-of-ten bites on hot ones; a live `anon`-callable payment RPC through two multi-finder reviews — the privilege hole a code-reading review structurally cannot see; the v0.8 close-session artefact failing on its own target class), one metric reversal (M7: 92 → 1, the R15 hook validated), one slot filled (R17, assertional coverage, four incidents in a single cycle all on payment surface), one promotion (R19 full, trigger rewritten to business temperature), four amendments, one candidate rejected as redundant, and the first mortality measurement of the corpus itself. The lesson the trail keeps: the mechanism ran in a different form than prescribed — agents in an afternoon, not a quarterly cron — and the doctrine now records that what matters is the falsifiable output, not fidelity to the imagined machinery.

**On the cadence.** Ten labelled versions across the arc's first hundred-odd days could be read as instability. The reading is reframed by the invariant every version has kept: each carries a named *fait nouveau* and a documented *retraction*. A doctrine that bumps for a new fact is applying axis 2 to itself; a doctrine that bumps without one would be the failure mode. The manifesto's *theory* is deliberately not rewritten every cadence — that would be ceremony — but v0.9 was a full resynchronisation, because the body had drifted far enough from the toolkit (it stopped at v0.4.1) that the gap had itself become the kind of stale Cache R2 and R18 exist to catch. v0.10 resynchronises again within twelve days — this time the lag was caught not by drift but by the toolkit's own footer flag (*"doc lag flagged, not fixed"*), the honesty mechanism doing its job: a named lag is a scheduled repair, an unnamed one is a lie in waiting.

## Critiques received

The doctrine applies axis 2 — *never revise on pushback without a new fact* — to itself, and it takes critique from two channels: external readers, and, from v0.6 on, an internal one.

### External critiques (v0.3 → v0.3.2)

Two external critiques on the published v0.3 were amended into the text within days. The **first** (14 May 2026) made three points: disproportionate theoretical armour (*"the operational value does not increase with the density of references"* — bibliography reorganised into load-bearing and consulted); self-protective wording that disqualified outside critique in advance (a *société de discours* move, sentence retired); and the rhetoric of cumulated GitHub stars as representativity (renamed to *falsifiable hypotheses*, scope bounded). Its closing line — *"Pick the rules. Leave the costume."* — is the v0.3.1 principle. The **second** (14 May 2026) produced an axis-by-axis rating and the verdict that the doctrine *"self-applies in declaration, not in verifiable practice"* — the sentence that, more than any other, set the trajectory: it is the direct ancestor of the v0.3.3 metric instrumentation and, two versions later, of R18. A third and fourth reading (an Anthropic auto-analysis, a Claude.ai web review) folded into the v0.4/v0.4.1 trail above.

### The internal critique — `/challenger` (v0.6 →)

From v0.6 on, the dominant critique channel stopped being external readers and became the doctrine's own falsification protocol, invoked *on the doctrine*. `/challenger` — the invocable instance of R4, extended by Am.R4 to audits and proposals — ran before each version shipped and repeatedly *demolished the author's own candidates*: three v0.7 proposals retracted as redundant; two v0.8 candidates collapsed into one and a third demoted; R18's strong premise refuted by a filesystem probe; two v0.9 supporting claims refuted while the core rule held; a v0.10 amendment candidate rejected as textually redundant, and the v0.10 audit falsifying the auditor's own archival probe mid-run. This is the deeper meaning of *the doctrine applies to itself* — the adversary of last resort is no longer an outside critic who may or may not read the next release, but a protocol the author is obliged to run against their own proposals before publishing them. A doctrine whose loudest critic is itself, by design, is the operational shape of axis 2 taken to its conclusion.

The textual side of every critique is closed by the version that received it; the empirical side — the metrics whose instrumentation is still the bottleneck, the R18 mechanisms prescribed but not yet wired, the framework-audit sample still bounded — is the standing open work, named here rather than hidden, because a doctrine that lists its own unfinished business is the only kind that can be trusted about the business it claims finished.
