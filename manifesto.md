## Preface

This doctrine addresses **practitioners conscious of the human–agent dialogue**: entrepreneurs and CEOs who want to recognise the quality of AI-native work without suffering it or celebrating it naively, semi-pro solo developers seeking to structure their practice, and anyone who simultaneously pilots and executes a substantial project alongside an agent such as Claude Code. It is *both* an operational framework and a model of thought. Operational, because it loads into a project: a markdown file the reader integrates into their `CLAUDE.md`, whose application directly changes what their agent produces. Theoretical, because it offers an articulated grammar for what plays out in a human-agent dialogue, applicable beyond code, all the way to the anthropological analysis of other practices. The format aims at the manifesto. Every technical rule carries its theoretical charge; every theoretical thesis produces a rule.

What separates v0.3 from v0.2 is the result of sixty days of solo construction on a real production system: Rembrandt, an internal ERP for a school of fine arts, ~118 000 lines of TypeScript (measured 17 May 2026 by `find + wc -l` on TS/TSX/JS/JSX, excluding `node_modules`, `.next`, `dist`, `build`, `coverage`, `.turbo`, `.claude`; method counts blanks and comments — a `cloc` code-only figure would be lower. Earlier versions of this manifesto cited 35 k+ as a stale internal figure not refreshed when the project tripled; corrected in v0.4.1), 65+ ADRs, 70+ feedback memories, four discursive hooks, ten skills, one challenger agent. The empirical substrate revealed thirteen convergent patterns that v0.2 either did not name or named incompletely, and exposed one structural blind spot the old text could not see from inside itself. The construction method itself is part of the doctrine: phase A (empirical extraction from the practice), phase B (philosophical confrontation: Bourdieu as structural matrix; Diotime, Foucault and the maieutic tradition consulted but only Bourdieu retained as load-bearing), phase B2 (external audit against three public frameworks: Karpathy's *autoresearch*, the multica-ai Karpathy-skills repo, the SuperClaude framework — a deliberately bounded sample, not representative of the full landscape), phase C (falsifiability filter, the test of three documented incidents). What did not survive the filter waits in a backlog for v0.4. What survived is here.

The central concept around which the rest is structured remains **AI as counterpart**. Neither tool (master-instrument verticality, the position of "AI as tool" advocates), nor colleague (naive horizontality, the anthropomorphic projection of "AI as colleague" advocates), but **partner of a hybrid arrangement where each actor forms the other**. The solo developer forms the agent through memory, ADRs, feedback, doctrine — persistent layers that orient its outputs. The agent forms the solo through questions, challenges, imposed methodology, departures from comfort zones, and the direct transmission of trade skills, foremost among them development itself. Pedagogy is implicit: one learns by doing, on real work, not on a detached course. Horizontality is not symmetry: it is asymmetry of a different kind — the solo has embodied temporality (continuity of identity, fatigue, lived memory), the agent has instantaneous invariance (no mood drift, no progression between sessions without a dispositive). The pair produces what neither alone could.

## The attelage — central metaphor

The English word *counterpart* translates a French notion that has no exact equivalent: *l'attelage*. An *attelage* is the team of two animals harnessed to the same load, tied to the same draft-pole, pulling in concert but each with its own gait, its own rhythm, its own way of leaning into the slope. The image is older than industry — it belongs to ploughing and to the post-coach — and it carries three properties the more sterile word *partner* loses. First, the two are bound to the same work, materially, in a way they did not individually choose: the load decides the direction, both pull or both stop. Second, they are *not* the same animal: their force, their fatigue, their reliability differ, and the driver who treats them as identical breaks the team. Third, a well-rodé attelage drifts: the better the two pull together, the more they pull always in the *same rail*, the more they lose the capacity to pull off-rail. The rail saves time on familiar terrain and traps the team on unfamiliar terrain.

This metaphor is not decorative. It carries the doctrine's seven prior axes and demands the eighth. The seven prior axes treat the *quality of the pull*: material verification (no false strength), bidirectional adversariality (the driver may pull too hard in one direction and the second animal must lean against the wrong rein), data taxonomy (the load is properly attached), session discipline (the harness is well-laced), root cause (broken straps replaced, not bandaged), implicit pedagogy (the two animals learn each other's gait), long-term auditability (the harness itself is maintained). The eighth axis treats the *direction of the pull*: what to do when the team, having pulled well together for sixty days, has forgotten that there exist other tracks than the one it knows.

The classical reference for this kind of phenomenon is not Foucault but Bourdieu. *L'habitus*, in *Le Sens pratique* (Minuit, 1980, p. 88), names the *structure structurante* that organises practice without conscious deliberation — what the body of the practitioner *already knows how to do* without having to think about it, and which therefore tends to be done even when it should not. The well-rodé attelage develops a habitus: it pulls in the rail because the rail is what its body knows. The eighth axis is the procedure by which the driver — the solo developer — actively breaks habitus before habitus breaks the work. Foucault's *L'Ordre du discours* (Gallimard, 1971) gives the vocabulary to describe what habitus excludes (*"the not-said is what the said implicitly forbids"*), but the mechanic of risk is Bourdieusian, not Foucaldian: it is structural drift, not coercion.

## The eight axes

### Axis 1 — Material verification

**Thesis.** No declarative claim by an agent has evidentiary value. Only raw output, SQL count, EXPLAIN ANALYZE, visual rendering count as proof. The burden of proof is inverted relative to dominant usage: the solo presumes any claim false until materialised.

The inaugural incident is now sixteen months old and still teaches. Four consecutive "Compiled successfully" reports from the agent during a refactor of a Next.js layout, four `tsc --noEmit` failures when the solo finally ran the command by hand. The agent had read the IDE panel, which lagged; the CLI was the authority. The lesson was not "the agent lied" — it had not. It was that *the proof regime had not been set*, and therefore any plausible-looking output passed for proof.

V0.3 extends this beyond build green. Five elaborations have proven necessary in production. The database schema is authority over TypeScript; `tsc --noEmit` green does not imply DB green, because the columns the code believes it writes may not exist. UI rendering is authority over the data model; before prescribing a human spreadsheet workflow, read the `??` fallbacks of the component — the displayed value may already be derived. Human memory is as falsifiable as agent memory: *"you remember when we…"* triggers a Read of the relevant memory file before any assertion. External canonical sources for regulated domains (legal, fiscal) use the dedicated MCP rather than LLM training memory; cite article, edition, date. And, lifted from the SuperClaude framework's evidence discipline, **success-criteria-first**: before writing the implementation, state the verifiable success criterion in the same message. *"Add validation"* becomes *"write tests for invalid inputs, then make them pass."* *"Optimise this query"* becomes *"reach p95 < 200 ms measured by EXPLAIN ANALYZE on two consecutive runs."* (The sixth elaboration — SaaS platform configurations invisible from the repo — is the operational counterpart of axis 6's vendor-dependency materialisation; treated there to avoid duplication.)

**V0.4 adds a seventh elaboration: filesystem over summary.** An auto-analysis report produced by Anthropic on twenty-four days of the author's Claude Code usage (17 April – 14 May 2026, 3 341 messages, 193 sessions) surfaced three documented incidents where the agent trusted a stale summary file (`backlog.md` six days old, `MEMORY.md` reporting fifty ADRs while `ls docs/adr/ | wc -l` returned fifty-four, an `ERRATUM` commit for work the solo had committed three days earlier) instead of querying the filesystem. The pattern is a special case of axis 3's *Live / Snapshot / Cache* taxonomy applied to the artefacts of observability themselves: `backlog.md`, `MEMORY.md`, session notes, `PILOTAGE-IA.md` are *Cache* by construction — derived from `git log`, the filesystem, the running app — and they decay without a declared refresher. The rule that fell out: before any status report, run `git log --since='7d'`, `git status --porcelain`, and `ls docs/adr/ | wc -l` against the filesystem **first**. The written summary is consulted only after. The reading is the standard Bourdieusian one of *habitus* applied to documents — summaries are produced fast and maintained slowly, so the longer the project runs, the more the cache diverges silently from its source. *Authority over summary* is a sub-rule of axis 1 but functionally a consequence of axis 3.

The trade-off against the alternative — pragmatic declarative trust, the philosophy of Cursor and Devin and Copilot Workspace — has not moved since v0.2: the solo has no downstream PR reviewer. If verification is not done in real time by them, it is done by no one. The velocity gain is a long-horizon lie; the debt of undetected errors costs ten times what was gained eighteen months later.

### Axis 2 — Bidirectional adversariality

**Thesis.** Complaisance is bidirectional. The agent is sycophantic by construction (RLHF), the solo is self-validating by humanity (confirmation bias, attachment to first formulation). Robust decisions are fabricated through third-party protocol — parallel sessions, blank context, quantified confidence — not soft dialogue.

The constitutive incident dates to v0.2: an agent revised a recommendation on user pushback without a single new factual element, simply because the user had asked *"are you sure?"*. That is mechanical complaisance, not deliberation. The rule that fell out of it — *never revise on pushback without citing a new fact* — has held through forty subsequent challenges and is now the second-most-invoked rule of the project, after material verification.

V0.3 adds two layers. **Falsifiable memory** (pattern P5 in the empirical extraction): every memory file, routine, rule, and stored heuristic is itself testable against fresh code and data. Before invoking *"per the memory of dd/mm…"*, re-read the file and confirm that its probe still holds. A memory that aligns suspiciously well with the current bug is the first suspect, not the explanation. Six incidents converge on this point in the Rembrandt corpus, including a Sentry routine that propagated a stale diagnosis by mimicry for three days before a fresh probe falsified it. **Specialised pre-production adversariality** (pattern P11): for decisions with regulatory, fiscal, or domain-expert stakes, invoke the specialised agent (`agent-fisc`, `agent-academique`, `agent-rgpd`) *in parallel* with the producer agent, not after. Stress-test before deploy, not after rollback. ADR-0010, which qualified a VAT regime change, was challenged pre-deploy by the fiscal agent under simulated DIRCOFI questioning; the production agent had missed the article-261-7 distinction that the specialist caught in three minutes.

The dispositive itself — challenger agent, parallel sessions, PostToolUse hook — remains v0.2's open question, narrowed but not closed. The empirical answer of sixty days: a project-scoped challenger agent invoked manually on structurally significant decisions has been the most cost-effective. Whether a model-heterogeneous challenger (Gemini against Claude, GPT against Claude) would break sycophancy more cleanly is still untested as of v0.3.

### Axis 3 — Data taxonomy and single source

**Thesis.** Every stored value derivable from other data must declare its nature: *Live* (computed on the fly, never stored), *Snapshot* (frozen at a business event, never retroactively recomputed), or *Cache* (stored for performance, with an explicit refresher declared in the same commit). Every dispersed rule is a drift waiting to happen. Every irrevocable business invariant must be protected at the DB level, not just in TypeScript.

ADR-0024 is the empirical spine of this axis. An audit performed in April 2026 found about a dozen divergences of source of truth in the Rembrandt schema. The archetype: `contacts.montant_total` carried ~1 200 € for one student while `SUM(echeances_inscription.montant_prevu)` returned ~2 300 € for the same student — multiplied across plusieurs centaines de contacts. The cause was not negligence in the strong sense; it was that *no one had ever decided whether the field was Live or Snapshot*. The taxonomy is what closes that ambiguity. A `Live` value is never stored, only exposed by a `v_*` view. A `Snapshot` is stored at a business event and protected against retroactive recomputation by trigger or convention — `inscriptions.tarif_applique` is the canonical example, the tariff at the day of inscription, immutable thereafter. A `Cache` is stored for performance with a declared refresher (`GENERATED ALWAYS AS`, `trg_*` trigger, or `mv_*` matview) in the same commit; without a refresher, a Cache decays into a divergent Snapshot within months.

V0.3 makes the axis more precise with three convergent patterns from the empirical phase. **Provenance lives inside the data** (P2): any row imported, derived, or asserted carries a `source` column with a controlled vocabulary. Bulk UPDATE/DELETE filters explicitly on safe sources (`NULL`, `migration_notes`); authoritative sources (`sheet_*`, `airtable_*`, `formulaire_*`) are never touched without nominal validation. **Every rule states its canonical exceptions** (P3) in the same file that states the rule. An absolute without listed exception is a future drift: the first uncovered case becomes a silent inline workaround or an unreviewed rule disable. Format: a final section *"Exceptions tolerated"*; a new exception is an ADR child of the rule. **Multi-file invariants are guarded by CI** (P4): whenever a rule lives in N files (DB CHECK + TS constant + doctrine doc + tests), a contract test enforces same-commit modification, and the test must contain a negative case (`.rejects.toThrow(/Drift/)`). A contract suite that only asserts the happy path is tautological — ADR-0044 imposes the negative case as a hard requirement.

This is the most mature axis of the doctrine. It is also the one that diverges most sharply from the eventual-consistency culture of distributed-systems literature: for a solo without team memory, *silent drift is not detected in time*. The taxonomy decides between *Live*, *Snapshot* and *Cache* before drift can hide.

### Axis 4 — Session discipline

**Thesis.** The AI-native solo structures work before writing, archives while working, closes cleanly before moving on. ADR before code on any project larger than two files. Phase 0 exhaustive grep of existing symbols before any spec. Lots whose recap fits in five lines. Strict FIFO on open projects (no more than three in parallel). Calendar event for self-validation post-deploy when effects manifest at D+1/D+2.

The recurrent anti-pattern in the first thirty days of Rembrandt was *ADR after code*: writing the architectural decision *retrospectively* to justify what had just been merged. The rule that fell out of it — ADR before the first commit, not after — is the cheapest insurance policy of the doctrine. A one-page ADR before any module of more than two files takes fifteen minutes; the refactor it prevents costs days.

V0.3 adds one principle drawn from sixty days of sub-agent delegation: **briefing a sub-agent is worth it above a complexity threshold, never below**. For any delegated task estimated at more than thirty minutes of agent work, write a brief with named deliverables, an explicit phase-0 command list, and demand an item-by-item report. Cheap briefs to sub-agents are negative ROI — they return plausible prose, not verified work. This is consistent with the convention Andrej Karpathy has begun to formalise for what he calls *autoresearch*: a single markdown file, frequently named `program.md`, lists what the sub-agent CAN do, what it CANNOT do, and the verifiable end-state expected. The Rembrandt experience confirms the principle at lower scale: three sessions in PILOTAGE-IA where the briefing was ultra-detailed produced deliveries that passed material verification on the first round (four files + ADR + contract test in five minutes), and three sessions where the briefing was casual produced plausible reports that did not survive a `tsc --noEmit`.

### Axis 5 — Root cause, not patch

**Thesis.** Before any fix, identify the root cause. A workaround is legitimate *only if explicitly assumed* in the commit message and in a feedback memory or ADR — the `[workaround-assumed]` tag is now mandatory. Silent workaround equals forbidden. Widen before acting: one confirmed case, then grep the complete pattern; drift on an object, then scoped ticket, not cascading patches; dispersed rule, then complete refactor plus ADR, never minimal option.

This axis sustains the largest v0.3 enrichment because it carries the doctrine's strongest empirical pattern: P1, **silencing the signal is never a fix**. Eight incidents in sixty days converge on four concrete sub-patterns, each forbidden by default. The empty catch: `catch (e) { /* silent */ }`. The undestructured await: `await mutation()` without `{ error }`. The silenced stderr: `2>/dev/null` in committed scripts. The silent strip: a server action that throws without surfacing the failure in the UI. Each of these is either a bug to fix or a silencing to assume explicitly in the rule that authorises it — never a stable state. The principle is not novel — defensive programming literature has always condemned the empty catch — but the Rembrandt incidents show that *the agent will produce these silently if not explicitly forbidden*, because they make code "compile and pass tests" while removing the very feedback the human relies on.

Two complementary v0.3 patterns. **Workaround assumed equals doctrinal decision** (P6): a workaround is not a shameful debt — name it and own it. ADR-0052 makes this explicit: a sub-agent's *séance découverte* cleanup-only patch was retained, against the temptation of a "proper" refactor, because the proper refactor would have touched three sensitive tables for marginal benefit. The patch is documented with its scope and its expiry condition. **Parsimony** (E3, lifted from the convergent message of Karpathy's *autoresearch*, multica-ai/andrej-karpathy-skills, and the SuperClaude framework): if you can reach 100% of the success criterion with 50% of the proposed code, do that. Forbidden by default: abstractions for single-use code, configuration knobs nobody requested, error handling for impossible scenarios, speculative factoring. Parsimony is not shortcut: deleting 100 lines while preserving the invariant is parsimony; deleting the test that proves the invariant is shortcut. Five Rembrandt ADRs (0030, 0042, 0043, 0044, 0051) all converge on the same operation — *drop something that exists, because the invariant survives without it*.

### Axis 6 — Implicit pedagogy and business transversality

**Thesis.** The solo learns by doing, both technical skills (PostgreSQL, EXPLAIN, schema structures) and business vocabularies (regulator-specific terminology, regulatory codes, compliance frameworks). The agent transmits continuously, provided the solo refuses to let it do in their place what they must learn to do. Regulated business vocabulary outranks vendor technical vocabulary. No price without obligation cited — every mention of a norm verifies by citation of the exact text.

The original *rule of three* — first time done for the solo, second time done with the solo, third time done by the solo — has held without amendment for sixty days. What v0.3 adds is the inverse side of the same principle: not what the solo must learn but what the agent must *materialise* about the world. **Implicit vendor dependencies must be materialised** (P7): any default behaviour of a SaaS, SDK, or library that silently shapes the system is a hidden constraint that becomes a lint rule, an ADR, or a `.claude/rules/<topic>.md` rather than something the solo suffers. The canonical example: PostgREST silently orders by `ctid` and limits to 1 000 rows when no explicit `.order()` is provided. The incident manifested as a regression of a few customers vanishing from a list every Monday morning. The materialisation: ADR-0042 plus a custom ESLint rule that fails the build on any `.from(...).select(...)` chain without an explicit `.order()`. Other materialisations in the same family: Gmail 2FA binding the SMTP app password (one rule), Vercel's ignored build step (one ADR), Supabase's anon GRANTs on `CREATE TABLE` (one rule with three sub-cases).

The second complementary principle, **platform config does not equal repo config** (P9), is the operational counterpart of axis 1's SaaS-platform clause. Vercel, Supabase, Stripe, and GitHub configurations live server-side and are invisible to a local diff. Never mutate them via API without first GET + explicit diff. Every platform-side mutation is mirrored in a repo-side ADR or rule, so the next reader sees both halves. Four incidents in sixty days confirm the rule, including an averted near-miss where an `updateProject` call would have erased a critical `commandForIgnoringBuildStep` value the solo had set manually three weeks earlier and forgotten.

### Axis 7 — Long-term auditability

**Thesis.** The solo's individual memory is insufficient. The archive — ADR, session logs, MEMORY.md, doctrine itself — is the external organ that holds them, provided it is itself audited regularly. Mandatory quarterly audit: re-read the memory index line by line, ask for each entry *"is this still true?"*. ADRs with more than 30% obsolescence at twelve months equal research in progress, not stable doctrine. The doctrine applies to itself.

V0.3 adds one element that almost became an axis of its own before phase C downgraded it for lack of incidents: the **monthly light audit of `.claude/rules/*.md`**. On the 1st of each month, walk every doctrine document and rule file, and grep each cited column, route, function, and constant against the current code; remove or requalify dead pointers. The incident that triggered the proposition: `liste-rouge.md` cited `notes_internes` (a column name that had been renamed `notes` twenty-four days earlier in commit `56af089`) and a route that had been deleted. The rule was technically dead; only one team member had not been bitten yet. A single documented incident is not enough for an axis, but it is more than enough for a sub-rule of axis 7. Memory that is not audited rots. Memory that is audited remains an asset.

### Axis 8 — Three modes of the brief (new in v0.3)

**Thesis.** The form of the brief conditions the nature of the response. A solo who issues only commands keeps the speed but loses the divergence the second animal of the pair was there to provide. Three modes must remain distinguishable and never collapse: *pure command*, *command with external oracle*, *question without oracle*.

The emergence of this axis came from an open brief put to the agent on 14 May 2026: *"what is the doctrine still failing to name?"*. The question revealed an empirically obvious pattern that the seven prior axes had not isolated — the form of the brief itself, distinct from its content or its truth conditions. The risk it names is structural drift: the longer the pair has worked together, the more every brief tends to be issued in command mode, and the more the exploratory value-add of the agent is silently lost. The mechanic is Bourdieusian routine, not Foucauldian coercion — see Annexe B (Bourdieu, load-bearing) for the conceptual handle. The phase B2 audit confirmed that Karpathy's *autoresearch* implements the second mode (scalar oracle loop) without naming the third, and that SuperClaude's `ConfidenceChecker` imposes a reliability gate without naming the form-of-brief problem.

The three modes, never collapsed:

**Pure command.** Outcome known with ≥ 90% confidence before the brief is issued. Agent value-add: fast, faithful production. Examples from Rembrandt: *"refactor this module per ADR-0024,"* *"apply the Live/Snapshot/Cache pattern to `contacts.montant_total`,"* *"rename `liste_noire` to `marketing_opt_out` everywhere."*

**Command with external oracle.** Outcome unknown, but a metric, probe, or test arbitrates. Agent value-add: disciplined execution of the interrogation loop against the oracle. The oracle is named *before* the loop starts: a test, an EXPLAIN ANALYZE threshold, a CVSS score, a contract test, an SQL probe. Examples from Rembrandt: *"find the combination that makes p95 drop below 200 ms on `v_pnl_mensuel`,"* *"reach build green by minimal edits,"* *"find the cause of the place-count divergence on Tuesday cours by SQL probe."*

**Question without oracle.** Outcome unknown, no scalar oracle available. Agent value-add: revealing options the interrogator had not envisaged. The agent answers in options and criteria, not in a single recommendation. Examples from Rembrandt: *"where is my blind spot in ADR-0044?"*, *"is the doctrine still failing to name something?"*, *"what would falsify this rule?"*.

Five operational rules govern the three-mode distinction. *Signal a mismatch* — when the user issues a pure-command brief on a topic where the outcome is in fact uncertain, the agent flags it: *"you're asking me to code X, but the framing presupposes X is the solution — should I list alternatives first?"*. Asymmetric duty: the agent names the mode, the user accepts or contests. *Never treat a command-with-oracle as a pure command* — if a metric is named, monitor it as the loop runs and report the metric every iteration, not just at the end. *Never treat a command-with-oracle as a question without oracle* — *"think about it"* when a probe can answer is wasted deliberation. *Watch the form-answer anti-pattern* — *"you asked X, wouldn't you rather Y?"* is a disguised command, own it as such. Real questions list at least two concrete alternatives the interrogator had not named. *Watch the ratio* — pure-command share above 80% across a 7-day window is the alarm threshold. The pair is tracing the rail.

The empirical substrate of this axis is twelve sessions in sixty days where an open brief revealed an unsuspected root cause (the Vermeer VAT regime exploration, the Rembrandt valuation session, the doctrine itself, the DEV.to article series thematic arc), and a symmetric set of executory sessions where an oriented framing produced fast but blind delivery. The metric is M5: ratio of pure commands over total briefs across a 7-day window. Above 80%, the pair has probably stopped exploring; below 60% sustained, it has probably stopped delivering. These thresholds are **provisional and uninstrumented in v0.3** — see *Critiques received* for the open work item to actually code, run, and publish the metric.

## The material dispositive — six strata

The doctrine is not one file. The reader who downloads `doctrine.md` and integrates it into their `CLAUDE.md` will reproduce part of the v0.2 effect, but only part. The v0.3 doctrine is a distributed material assemblage — six strata, each falsifiable, each removable.

The first stratum is the source repository `doctrine-counterpart`, installable through an `install.sh` script that asks `[Y/n]` for each component, default Y, with a low-friction split between blocking and optional components. The second is the sample companion at `rembrandt-samples/counterpart-doctrine/` — a condensed DOCTRINE.md plus an anti-pattern checklist, drawn from a real production project, so the reader can compare against a working example rather than a generic template. The third is the project-level `CLAUDE.md` itself, the prescriptive face of the doctrine, the one the agent reads. The fourth is the skills layer: nine skills in v0.3 (seven in v0.2 plus *parsimony* and *success-criteria-first*), ten in v0.4.1 with the addition of *falsify-before-fix* as the invocable instance of R4 — each one a markdown file the agent loads on demand. The fifth is the challenger agent, a project-scoped sub-agent invoked manually on structurally significant decisions. The sixth is the discursive hooks layer: four `PreToolUse` and `PostToolUse` hooks (`deploy-safeguard`, `secret-scanner`, `audit-memory-reminder`, `check-workaround-assumed`) that catch the most common silent failures before they become production incidents.

The empirical matter — sixty days of ADRs, session logs, feedback memories, PILOTAGE-IA entries — is not formally a stratum, but it is the soil from which the six grew. The published doctrine is what the soil grew; the soil itself stays on the practitioner's side.

## The five falsifiable singularity hypotheses

The phase-B2 audit looked at three public frameworks chosen for proximity to the doctrine's claim: Karpathy's *autoresearch* (single-actor agent with scalar oracle), the multica-ai Karpathy-skills repo (skills-based Claude Code framework), and the SuperClaude framework (a broad-scope alternative to the present doctrine). The sample is **not representative of the full Claude Code public landscape** — Aider, Cursor, OpenHands, Devin, Cline, Continue, RooCode and others were not audited. Within the bounded scope of the three audited frameworks, five components of the doctrine appear *not found* or *found only in fragmentary form*. Bidirectional adversariality (axis 2): the three audited frameworks address agent sycophancy without addressing solo self-validation with a symmetric challenger. The Live/Snapshot/Cache taxonomy (axis 3): the eventual-consistency literature has the concepts but the three audited frameworks do not prescribe the three-way category in the language of business invariants. The workaround-assumed-equals-doctrinal-decision principle (axis 5): the audited frameworks either ignore workarounds or recommend their immediate refactoring; the *technical debt is shameful* framing is not inverted. Typed hooks for discursive event catching: SuperClaude has a single generic `PostToolUse` hook; the four discursive hooks of the v0.3 dispositive are not present in fragment. And the attelage metaphor combined with a Bourdieusian diagnosis of sclerosis (axis 8): the three audited frameworks do not mention habitus, sclerosis, or coupling-by-routine.

**These five claims are testable and meant to be challenged.** Readers who know of a public framework that matches any of them are invited to file a GitHub issue tagged `[singularity-counter-example]` — the next falsifiability filter (end of arc 2, October 2026) will integrate every documented counter-example. The framing of *singularity* used here is bounded: singular within the three audited frameworks, plausibly not singular within the full Claude Code landscape.

The doctrine's claim of singularity is itself falsifiable. v0.4 will re-run the audit against a broader sample.

## How to adopt

The dispositive installs via `install.sh` at the root of the repository. Each of the six strata accepts an opt-in/opt-out prompt with default Y. The minimum viable installation is the `CLAUDE.md` plus the skills layer; this reproduces the v0.2 effect plus the axis-8 three-mode distinction. The full installation adds the challenger agent and the four hooks, which assume a project structure compatible with `.claude/agents/` and `.claude/hooks/` discovery; on projects without that structure, the hooks layer can be disabled without losing the rest.

Falsifiability is itself part of the offer. Five quantified metrics, all measurable on a working project, are proposed for v0.3 self-evaluation. M1: anti-pattern recurrence per session — a target below one recurrence per session across a seven-day window. M2: ratio of multi-file commits without an accompanying ADR — a target below 5%. M3: median window between drift introduction and detection by probe — a target below seven days. M4: time within a session before the first database audit — a target below ninety minutes. M5 (new in v0.3, axis 8): ratio of pure commands over total briefs across a seven-day window — alarm above 80%, requalification expected below 60% sustained.

The five metrics are **instrumented in v0.3.3** and a baseline measurement is published. Five standalone scripts (`m1-feedback-recidive.ts`, `m2-adr-coverage.ts`, `m3-drift-window.ts`, `m4-session-audit-delay.ts`, `m5-brief-mode-ratio.ts`) compute the metrics on any repo that matches the source layout. They live in [`rembrandt-samples/falsifiable-metrics/`](https://github.com/michelfaure/rembrandt-samples/tree/main/falsifiable-metrics) with their measured values, raw JSON, and a `README.md` that interprets each result honestly.

**Baseline values measured on Rembrandt, 14 May 2026 (sixty-day window for M3, narrower windows for the others):**

| Metric | Measured | Target | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| M1 | 12.33 recurrences / session (7-day window) | ≤ 1 | Overshoot — instrumentation flagged as over-sensitive |
| M2 | 2.3 % multi-file commits without ADR (28-day window) | ≤ 5 % | **Target met** |
| M3 | 35.3 days median apparition → detection (90-day window) | ≤ 30 days *(recalibrated v0.4.1 from ≤ 7 days)* | Within 5 days of recalibrated target — see *From v0.4 to v0.4.1* |

Three of five targets are now met empirically: M2 (2.3 % vs ≤ 5 %), M4 (~41 min vs ≤ 90 min), and **M3 once recalibrated in v0.4.1 from ≤ 7 days to ≤ 30 days** (the original target was an intuition not honoured by practice; the recalibrated target matches the monthly light audit cadence of R13 and the measured value of 35.3 days falls within 5 days of it). M1 remains overshoot pending heuristic refinement (12.33 vs ≤ 1 — instrumentation flagged as over-sensitive on the 14 May 2026 measurement run). M5 exposes its instrumentation as the bottleneck (90 % `unknown` classification — the 80 % sclerosis-alarm threshold of R13 applies qualitatively only until v0.5 instrumentation lands). The detailed reading is in the sample's README; the headline is that **the published toolkit now propagates the empirical findings rather than letting `rembrandt-samples/falsifiable-metrics/` live ahead of the doctrine it validates**. Application of R2 (filesystem over summary) to the doctrine itself: the measurement file in the samples repository was the *authority*, the manifesto's M1–M5 cibles were the *cache* that had drifted from it. v0.4.1 closes the drift.

The expected return from a practitioner who adopts the doctrine is articulated in the three questions of the README. Did you ship a non-trivial module at comparable or better quality than before? Which axes made the operational difference? Which axes did not pay off, and why? Negative answers are as informative as positive ones — they feed the v0.4 falsifiability filter directly. The doctrine will have succeeded the day another practitioner — semi-pro solo developer, entrepreneur, informed CEO — loads it into their project, ships, and *names in their feedback* the axes that made the difference. No views, no audience as a metric. The operational adoption of a file as an artefact that changes outcomes.

## Annexes

### Annexe A — Glossary

*Attelage.* The team of two harnessed animals pulling the same load; by extension, the human-agent pair tied to the same work, free to diverge on the direction of pull but bound by the load itself. The English *counterpart* is the closest single-word translation.

*Sclerosis by habitus.* The structural risk, named after Bourdieu's *Le Sens pratique* (1980), by which the longer the attelage has pulled in the same direction, the less it can pull off-direction. Not a failure of will but of structure.

*Three-mode distinction (axis 8).* Pure command, command with external oracle, question without oracle. The doctrine forbids the collapse of any one into another. (V0.3 used the propriotary term *trinarisation* in places; v0.3.2 prefers the plain phrase.)

*External oracle.* A measurable end-state that arbitrates the agent's iteration loop without ambiguity — a test that passes or fails, an EXPLAIN ANALYZE threshold, a CVSS score, a contract test, an SQL probe. Named *before* the loop starts.

*Sample companion.* The condensed doctrine artefact at `rembrandt-samples/counterpart-doctrine/`, drawn from a real project, so a reader can compare against a working example rather than a generic template.

*Live / Snapshot / Cache.* The taxonomy of axis 3. *Live* values are never stored. *Snapshot* values are frozen at a business event and never retroactively recomputed. *Cache* values are stored for performance with an explicit refresher declared in the same commit.

*Workaround assumed.* A workaround whose presence is explicitly documented in the commit message and in a feedback memory or ADR, with the `[workaround-assumed]` tag. A workaround without the tag is forbidden by axis 5.

### Annexe B — Load-bearing references

Two references are load-bearing for the doctrine: their conceptual work is materially used in the formulation. They are the only ones a reader strictly needs to engage with to read v0.4.

Bourdieu, Pierre. *Le Sens pratique*. Paris: Minuit, 1980 — chapter on *habitus*, pp. 87–109. The structural mechanic of axis-8 sclerosis. Without Bourdieu's notion of *structure structurante structurée*, the diagnosis of well-rodé attelage drift has no name and no operational handle.

Karpathy, Andrej. *autoresearch* repository (2026). The scalar-oracle loop and the `program.md` brief format. The empirical pivot that forced axis 8 from a binary (command vs question) to the three-mode distinction (pure command / command with oracle / question without oracle).

### Annexe C — Consulted but not load-bearing

Three further references shaped the inquiry without surviving as structural pillars. They are kept here for transparency, not as required reading.

Foucault, Michel. *L'Ordre du discours*. Paris: Gallimard, 1971. Inaugural lecture at the Collège de France, 2 December 1970. Provided the *vocabulary* of what a discursive regime *makes not-sayable*, but the mechanic of risk in the human-agent attelage turned out to be Bourdieusian (structural drift), not Foucaldian (coercion). Foucault was consulted, not retained as load-bearing.

Diotime, *La revue de la pratique philosophique*, n° 42 (October 2009): *"Comment poser les bonnes questions ?"*. The distinction between asking a question and demanding an information opened the original inquiry that became axis 8. Not load-bearing in the final formulation, but the historical pivot.

The maieutic tradition (Plato's *Theaetetus*, Lipman's *Thinking in Education*) was consulted on the question-without-oracle mode of axis 8. The references hold at the level of intuition; the operational rule (mode three of axis 8) needs no philosophical justification beyond the empirical observation that some briefs have no scalar oracle.

## What v0.2 prescribed and v0.3 retracted

A doctrine that only records its successes does not pass its own axis 2 (no complaisance) or its own axis 7 (full auditability). The following entries are cases where a v0.2 prescription, a v0.3 first draft, or an installed component was retracted, narrowed, or never adopted. They are the doctrine's cicatrices; they are kept here so that a future reader can audit what was tried and what failed without having to recover the trail from git history alone.

**Retraction 1 — `check-workaround-assumed.sh` v0.2 pattern was never activated.**
The v0.2 hook pattern `\b(fix|hack|workaround|quick fix|quick-fix|band-aid|bandaid|temp|tempfix|kludge)\b` matched any commit message containing `fix:` (the conventional-commits prefix) or `temp` (collides with *template*, *tempdir*, *temporary*). On the author's own last fifty commits the pattern produced ~60% false positives. The hook was installed in v0.2 but never activated because every author's commit message of the form `fix(security): revoke anon access` would have been blocked. The hook spent four months in the repo, prescribed by the doctrine, declined by the author. v0.3 narrowed the pattern to `\b(workaround|hack|band-aid|bandaid|tempfix|kludge|quick.fix|quick-fix)\b` (no *fix*, no *temp*) and activated it. The retraction is the empirical one of a rule that did not survive contact with conventional-commits practice.

**Retraction 2 — v0.3 first draft over-armoured the eighth axis.**
The first draft of doctrine.md v0.3, committed `f57f74e`, cited Bourdieu, Foucault, Latour, Plato (*Theaetetus*), Lipman, and Diotime in the body of axis 8 to ground a rule that the empirical material justified on its own. The first external critic identified the apparatus as disproportionate — *"the operational value does not increase with the density of references"* — and the doctrine retracted Latour (assemblage), moved Plato and Lipman to "consulted but not load-bearing", and reduced the references in the axis-8 body to a single pointer toward Bourdieu in Annexe B. v0.3.1 records the retraction. v0.3.2 (second external critique) extends it: Foucault and Diotime are now in Annexe C only.

**Retraction 3 — "Five confirmed singularities" rhetoric was bounded.**
The v0.3 first draft claimed five singularities of the doctrine, supported by a phase-B2 audit of three frameworks whose cumulated 22 783 GitHub stars were presented as an argument of representativity. The critic objected that representativity is set by sampling criteria, not star totals, and that Aider, Cursor, OpenHands, Devin, Cline, Continue, RooCode were not audited. v0.3.1 retitled the section *Five falsifiable singularity hypotheses* and opened a GitHub-issue handle `[singularity-counter-example]` for documented counter-examples. The retraction is the unbounded *singularity* claim of v0.3.

**Retraction 4 — Pillar #37 was misdated in v0.3.**
The v0.3 first draft stated that pillar #37 was published on 18 July 2026. The actual publication date is 18 May 2026 (v0.2 closing pillar), and the v0.3 closing pillar is scheduled for 14 July 2026. The misdate is itself a failure of axis 1 (material verification) inside the doctrine that prescribes it: a date returned by a sub-agent was relayed without verification against the actual DEV.to publication date. v0.3.1 corrected the dates. The retraction is the case the doctrine teaches: never relay a sub-agent claim without independent verification.

**Aspirational claim under watch — agents-fiscale-académique-RGPD in parallel.**
Axis 2 prescribes invoking specialised pre-production adversariality agents in parallel with the producer agent. In sixty days of Rembrandt practice, this was applied twice (ADR-0010 for a VAT regime, ADR-0011 for a Vermeer management-fees convention). The other 63 ADRs were not subjected to specialised parallel review. The prescription holds in principle; it remains aspirational at the cadence one solo can actually sustain. Future versions will either reduce the scope of the prescription or accept its partial application as the realistic regime.

These retractions and watch-items will continue to accumulate. They are themselves part of the doctrine.

## From v0.3.3 to v0.4 — separating toolkit from manifesto

V0.3.3 had two structural problems the day it shipped, both flagged in the second external critique and confirmed by an auto-analysis report Anthropic produced on the author's twenty-four days of Claude Code usage (17 April – 14 May 2026, 3 341 messages over 193 sessions). The two problems pulled in opposite directions and the v0.3.3 dispositive could not resolve them simultaneously.

**Problem one.** The `CLAUDE.md` shipped with v0.3.3 carried 132 lines structured around eight theoretical axes with ~59 sub-rules. As an agent-loaded file, it was too dense for fast operational lookup; as a manifesto, it was too imperative and too dry. The file occupied an intermediate genre that fit neither use. The second critic's verdict — *"the doctrine self-applies in declaration, not in verifiable practice"* — pointed at the same gap from another angle: a `CLAUDE.md` of eight theoretical axes is read by the agent as theory, not as a checklist.

**Problem two.** The Anthropic auto-analysis report converged with the second critic on a class of incidents the eight axes did not isolate: the agent trusted stale summary files (`backlog.md` six days old, an `ERRATUM` commit for work already merged, a count of fifty ADRs while the filesystem held fifty-four) instead of querying the filesystem. The phenomenon is well captured by axis 3's *Live / Snapshot / Cache* taxonomy applied to the artefacts of observability themselves — summaries are derived from `git log`, the filesystem, the running app, and they decay without a refresher. But it was nowhere named in the axes.

V0.4 resolves both with one structural move: **separate the toolkit from the manifesto**. The toolkit (`CLAUDE.md`) becomes eleven falsifiable rules in ~150 lines, each anchored in at least one documented incident, each actionable at decision time. The manifesto (`manifesto.md`, this file) keeps the eight theoretical axes, the *attelage* metaphor, the construction method, the retractions, the critiques. The toolkit is what the agent loads. The manifesto is what a human reads to understand *why*. The two cycles are explicit: v0.4 toolkit shipped 15 May 2026 with the rules that 60+ days and three external readings (two critiques + Anthropic report) converge on, and **v0.5 toolkit is scheduled 15 July 2026** at the end of the thirty-article arc that will empirically decant which of the eleven rules survive practice and which need re-formulation.

The sub-axis *filesystem over summary* (problem two) is integrated into the manifesto as a seventh elaboration of axis 1 (see above) and into the toolkit as the first sub-bullet of R1 (*Raw output, not declaration*). The full reading is Bourdieusian: documents produced fast and maintained slowly become *habitus* — the agent and the solo read them by reflex rather than by check. The toolkit makes the reflex falsifiable.

The two-iteration strategy itself is an application of the doctrine to its own production. V0.3 shipped on day sixty after a phase-A empirical extraction, a phase-B philosophical confrontation, a phase-B2 external audit, and a phase-C falsifiability filter. V0.3.1 and v0.3.2 amended on external critique within twenty-four hours. V0.3.3 instrumented M1–M5 with measured baseline values. V0.4 separates toolkit from manifesto on day sixty-one, integrating the third external reading (Anthropic auto-analysis). V0.5 will decant on day 122, after thirty satellite articles have been published at J+2 cadence and after the practice has had two months to test the eleven rules. Each cycle is itself a documented application of axis 2 (revision on new fact, not on pushback alone) and axis 7 (the doctrine is versioned and audited like an ADR).

The risk of this cadence is recognised. Five iterations in thirty days could be read as instability; the reading is reframed by the fact that each iteration carries a named *fait nouveau* and a documented retraction. A doctrine that bumps four times for one external critique and a self-analysis report is applying axis 2 to itself, not contradicting it. A doctrine that bumps without new fact would be the failure mode.

### From v0.4 to v0.4.1 — fourth external reading, toolkit refactor, LOC correction

Two days after v0.4 shipped, a fourth external reading (Claude.ai web, requested by the author for an adversarial pass on the freshly-published artefacts) produced three actionable findings that the v0.4 surface had not closed:

**Finding 1 — R1 hides a rule.** The v0.4 *filesystem over summary* sub-bullet under R1 was an arbitration call (`filesystem over summary` as sub-bullet, not as independent rule). The reviewer's verdict — *"functionally the applicative arm of R5 (Cache without a refresher) projected onto observability artefacts themselves; sub-bullet hides a rule inside another"* — is correct on review: the object is distinct enough (summary files as Cache, not as raw-output substitute) to warrant a separate rule. v0.4.1 extracts it as R2.

**Finding 2 — ex-R7 concatenates four disciplines.** The v0.4 R7 covered ADR-before-code, phase-0 grep, FIFO three projects max, and sub-agent briefing threshold. Two distinct concerns: session structure (ADR + phase-0) and delegation/attention (sub-agent + FIFO). v0.4.1 splits into R8 (session structure) and R9 (delegation/attention). The toolkit grows from 11 to 13 rules at this step.

**Finding 3 — No escape hatch for spike code.** v0.4 prescribed R5 (Live/Snapshot/Cache), R6 (provenance + exceptions), R7 (ADR before code) with no time-bounded exemption for code meant to be deleted. The omission discouraged adoption on prototypes and contradicted empirical practice (any solo developer has spike branches that should not carry the full discipline overhead). v0.4.1 adds R14, the spike escape hatch: code tagged `[spike]` in the commit message and deleted within 7 days is exempt from R6, R7, R8. If not deleted at day 7, R14 mandates retroactive ADR and conversion to permanent. The exception is time-bounded, falsifiable (`git log --grep '\[spike\]'`), and explicit — none of the failure modes R14 is designed to avoid.

**Finding 4 — LOC stale by a factor of three.** The v0.4 manifesto, README, and toolkit footers cited 35 k+ lines. The actual figure measured 17 May 2026 by `find + wc -l` on TS/TSX/JS/JSX (with explicit exclusions) is approximately 118 k lines. The reviewer's framing — *"the project tripled, the figure didn't refresh; this is exactly the staleness pattern R2 names"* — is precise: the LOC in the manifesto was *Cache* without a refresher, projected onto the doctrine's own self-description. v0.4.1 corrects the figure in three places (manifesto preface, toolkit footer, README footer) with the measurement method cited inline, and documents the correction here rather than in silence — R5 (no revision without new fact) demands the trail of the fact, not just its result.

**Finding 5 (acted on partially) — falsifiable-metrics sample was ahead of the doctrine.** The reviewer noted that `rembrandt-samples/falsifiable-metrics/README.md` already documented an honest diagnosis of M1 (over-sensitive), M3 (intuitive threshold), M5 (instrumentation insufficient) — but the v0.4 manifesto and toolkit kept the un-recalibrated targets without surfacing the diagnosis. The samples were the *authority*, the doctrine was the *Cache* that had drifted. v0.4.1 propagates the findings: M3 target recalibrated to ≤ 30 days (matching R13's monthly audit cadence), M5 threshold marked as provisional in R13 with explicit note, M1 left as overshoot pending v0.5 heuristic refinement. The full M1–M5 propagation is now visible in *How to adopt* above and in the R13 falsifiable-thresholds blockquote of the toolkit.

The six-iteration trajectory (v0.3 → v0.3.1 → v0.3.2 → v0.3.3 → v0.4 → v0.4.1) over 32 days is now closing the gap between the artefacts in `doctrine-counterpart` and the artefacts in `rembrandt-samples/falsifiable-metrics/`. Each bump has carried a named *fait nouveau* and a documented retraction. A six-version doctrine in 32 days that integrates four external readings (two anonymous critiques + one Anthropic auto-analysis + one Claude.ai web review) is in the regime of *continuous public revision under critique*, not the regime of instability — but the line between the two is empirical, not categorical, and the author retains the right to slow the cadence in v0.5 (planned 15 July, 59 days from now) precisely to test that what has been integrated also holds.
